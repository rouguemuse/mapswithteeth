# TEXAS POLICY BRIEF: CROSS-SYSTEM CONTINUITY INFRASTRUCTURE
**Standard**: Version 0.1 — Discussion Draft (October 2026)  
**Target Audience**: Texas Legislators, Sunset Advisory Commission Staff, State & County Agency Leadership, Judicial Administrators  
**Subject**: Addressing Information Seam Loss Across Child Safety, Family Violence, and Court Systems in Texas  
**Contact**: `policy@mapswithteeth.org` | Maps With Teeth Public-Interest Technology Initiative  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A person navigating family violence, stalking, or child-safety crises in Texas routinely interacts with up to five distinct public and nonprofit institutions: municipal police, county sheriffs, child welfare (DFPS/CPS), civil family courts, and victim services. 

Under current administrative practices, each institution acts on an isolated fragment. Context evaporates at institutional and county boundaries, leaving the survivor to act as the sole integration layer. When related matters fail to connect across systems, public agencies experience preventable investigative delays, repeated intake costs, and unmitigated safety risks.

> **Central Finding**: *The problem is not always that information does not exist. Sometimes it exists in five places, under five identifiers, with five separate decision-makers, and no mechanism requiring anyone to recognize the relationship between them.*  
> *Maps With Teeth does not propose merging those five systems. It asks what minimum continuity must survive when responsibility crosses from one to another.*

## 1. TEXAS PRECEDENT: MULTIDISCIPLINARY CONTINUITY ALREADY EXISTS

Maps With Teeth is **not proposing that Texas invent multidisciplinary continuity.** Texas already relies on formalized cross-system collaboration in several high-stakes areas:

| Texas Legal Authority | Statutory Mechanism | Multidisciplinary Precedent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tex. Fam. Code §§ 261.301(f), 261.3011** | Joint DFPS & Law Enforcement Investigations | Mandates joint investigation guidelines, role clarity, mutual agreements, and collaborative training. |
| **Tex. Fam. Code § 264.403** | Children’s Advocacy Center (CAC) MOUs | Mandates formal interagency contracts among DFPS, local police, and prosecutors. |
| **Tex. Fam. Code § 264.4031** | CAC Multidisciplinary Working Protocols | Establishes working protocols for referral criteria, intake processes, timely information exchange, and case tracking. |
| **Tex. Fam. Code § 264.406 & § 264.406(e)** | Multidisciplinary Teams (MDTs) & Information Sharing | Requires recurring cumulative case review and authorizes lawful, bounded exchange of confidential records. |
| **Tex. Gov't Code §§ 791.003, 791.011** | Interlocal Cooperation Act | Authorizes local governments and state agencies to contract for shared administrative and public-safety functions. |
| **Tex. Fam. Code § 86.0011; CCP Art. 7B.104** | Statewide Protective Order Transmission | Demonstrates existing statewide electronic infrastructure for transmitting protective order data to law enforcement. |
| **Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Art. 5.06** | Retrievability of Family-Violence Reports | Requires law enforcement agencies to maintain retrievable family violence documentation accessible to officers. |

**The Policy Gap**: Texas mandates multidisciplinary continuity within Children’s Advocacy Centers and specialized High-Risk Teams, but offers **zero standardized continuity protocols** when a survivor or family unit moves across standard frontline desks, county lines, civil protective order courts, school districts, or utility providers.

## 2. THE SIX CORE CONTINUITY MODULES (STANDARD v0.1)

The Cross-System Continuity Standard (v0.1) provides a lightweight, anti-surveillance protocol structured into six modular interventions:

1. **Module 01: Linked-Matter Review** (`PROPOSED`)  
   *Purpose*: Prevent potentially relevant matters from becoming invisible solely because another authorized system holds them.  
   *Rule*: Strictly non-prejudicial. *Related does not mean proven.* Transmits only a presence signal prompting authorized file requests under existing law.
2. **Module 02: Closed-Loop Referrals** (`PROTOTYPE`)  
   *Purpose*: Prevent referrals from ending at "sent."  
   *Rule*: Requires receiving entity to acknowledge receipt and log an administrative disposition within 48 hours.
3. **Module 03: Continuity Receipt** (`IMPLEMENTED`)  
   *Purpose*: Preserve the administrative path.  
   *Rule*: Client-side cryptographic receipt held directly by the participant. Agency acknowledgment verifies only specific checked administrative facts, never the broader narrative.
4. **Module 04: Cumulative Review Triggers** (`PROPOSED`)  
   *Purpose*: Identify when isolated handling obscures escalating risk across systems.  
   *Rule*: Absolutely not a numerical AI risk score. Transparent encounter thresholds trigger human supervisory or multidisciplinary review.
5. **Module 05: Decision Ownership** (`PROTOTYPE`)  
   *Purpose*: Eliminate unassigned responsibility.  
   *Rule*: At every handoff, explicitly answers: *Who owns the next action?* (Not "who owns the entire case").
6. **Module 06: Context Before Closure** (`PROPOSED`)  
   *Purpose*: Track process completeness before case exit.  
   *Rule*: Pre-closure administrative checklist records what was reviewed, what was unavailable, and closing rationale without forcing substantive findings.

## 3. PROPOSED 90-DAY CONTINUITY MECHANICS PILOT

Rather than launching a sprawling multi-agency mandate on Day 1, Maps With Teeth proposes a narrow, low-risk operational pilot:

```
[Referral Dispatched] ───► [Continuity Receipt] ───► [Electronic Acknowledgment] ───► [Decision Owner Bound] ───► [Disposition]
```

- **Scope**: 2–3 participating organizations (e.g., Victim-Service Navigator $\rightarrow$ Civil Legal Aid Intake).
- **Data Safeguard**: **Phase 1 uses 100% synthetic demonstration data (0% live survivor PII).**
- **Core Measurement Metrics**:
  - *Acknowledgment Rate*: Did someone confirm receipt? (Target $>95\%$)
  - *Time to Acknowledgment*: Latency before signoff (Target $<48$ hours)
  - *Decision-Owner Rate*: Is one responsible next actor identified? (Target $100\%$)
  - *Lost-Handoff Rate*: Referrals unacknowledged after 14 days (Target $0\%$)

The Department of Family and Protective Services (DFPS) is currently undergoing Sunset Review with public testimony scheduled for **November 18, 2026**.

The Texas Sunset Advisory Commission and legislative leadership have an immediate opportunity to integrate closed-loop referral accountability and context-before-closure audit requirements into Sunset statutory directives, establishing baseline continuity without expanding agency headcount or creating centralized surveillance databases.
